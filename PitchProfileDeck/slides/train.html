How we trained the models we own

Fine-tuned

Identity model: CLIP ViT-B/16

Starts from: OpenAI CLIP ViT-B/16 weights.

Learns from: 48 FOOTPASS games: 6.0 million player thumbnails with true numbers, identities and goalkeeper flags.

Learns to: Read shirt digits, tell players apart and spot goalkeepers. Digits are learned only from readable thumbnails.

Checked on: 3 unseen FOOTPASS games, 13,610 tracklets.

Cost: 10,000 steps on one RTX 3090.

Fine-tuned

Action spotter: T-DEED

Starts from: The SoccerNet 2025 T-DEED baseline.

Learns from: SoccerNet ball-action games plus 48 FOOTPASS games, some with partial labels.

Learns to: Find 12 kinds of action in the video, with extra weight on rare ones such as tackles and blocks.

Checked on: 2 unseen SoccerNet games and 3 unseen FOOTPASS games.

Cost: 3,000 steps, about 3 hours on one RTX 3090.

About 70 seconds. Most models are used as released, so only two of the models in use are ours. Fine-tuned means we started from published weights and kept training on our data. Identity model: we started from OpenAI CLIP and trained on 48 FOOTPASS games, about six million player thumbnails with true shirt numbers, identities and goalkeeper flags. It learns to read the two digits of a shirt number, to tell players apart, and to spot goalkeepers. The key trick is that digits are learned only from thumbnails where the number is readable; otherwise the model learns to guess the number from how the player looks. It was checked on three unseen FOOTPASS games and took 10,000 steps on one RTX 3090. Action spotter: we started from the SoccerNet 2025 T-DEED baseline and trained on SoccerNet ball-action games plus the 48 FOOTPASS games, some with partial labels. It learns to find 12 kinds of action in the video, with extra weight on rare ones such as tackles and blocks. It was checked on two unseen SoccerNet games and three unseen FOOTPASS games and took about three hours.
