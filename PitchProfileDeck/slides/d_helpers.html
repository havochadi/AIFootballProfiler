3 · Problem 4 · teams, direction, ball

Which team, which way, and where is the ball?

The problem

A detection says “player”, not which side. Nothing says which way a team attacks, and the ball is missed in more than half of the frames where it is visible, with false candidates among the hits.

Our answer

Hand-written

K-means, a defending rule and a path search

Data it uses: Torso colours of detected players, where the players stand, and ball candidates.

Trained on: Nothing. Hand-written, no training data.

How it works

1

Teams: two-cluster K-means on torso colour, with grass masked out.

2

Direction: defenders stay between ball and own goal, so the smaller mean position is the left goal.

3

Ball path: a shortest-path search through the ball candidates of each shot.

Outcome

97%

of detected players on the right team

Attack direction is right on all 8 halves that could be checked. The ball is located in a median 64% of live play.

Three hand-written algorithms, so no training data is needed. Outcome: 97% of detected players on the right team. Attack direction is right on all 8 halves that could be checked. The ball is located in a median 64% of live play. More detail. Teams: Median torso colour in CIELAB, with grass pixels masked, then two-cluster K-means with 12 random starts. Referees keep the detector’s class. The two clusters stay anonymous until a person names them. Attack direction: Defenders stay between the ball and their own goal, so over a half the team with the smaller mean x defends the left goal. A gap of at least 1.5 m counts. Right on all 8 halves where kick-off could be checked. Ball path: A shortest-path search over the ball candidates of each camera shot. Costs: −0.5 ln(confidence) per candidate, 1.0 per skipped sample, 4.0 per new piece. Steps limited to 90 px and 38 m/s. Ball located in a median 64% of live play. Note: Three small algorithms with no training: boxes and candidates in, two teams, an attack direction and one ball out.
