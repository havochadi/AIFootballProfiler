How do we find every player and the ball?

The problem

A broadcast frame holds about 14 outfield players, goalkeepers, referees and a ball only a few pixels wide. Every later step depends on finding them first.

Our answer

Used as released

YOLOv8x detectors

Data it uses: Video frames, 12.5 a second, resized to 1,280 px wide.

Trained on: Roboflow football images of players, goalkeepers, referees and the ball. Used as released.

How it works

1

Resize the frame to 1,280 px and pass it once through the network.

2

Predict a box and a class at three grid scales; merge overlapping boxes.

3

Keep boxes at 0.1 confidence or more. Later stages remove the false ones.

Outcome

89.7%

of visible player time detected

Weakest on close-ups: 63.6% for boxes over 150 px tall. About 14 outfield players are found in each frame.

Step 1: The frame is resized to 1,280 pixels wide and passed once through the network. Step 2: At three grid scales it predicts a box and a class score for each location: player, goalkeeper, referee or ball. Step 3: Overlapping boxes of one object are merged (non-maximum suppression, IoU 0.5). Step 4: Boxes with confidence of at least 0.1 are kept. The low bar is deliberate: later stages remove the false ones. In → out: A frame → labelled boxes. Origin: Roboflow football detector weights, used without retraining. A second ball-only YOLOv8x runs when the first finds no ball. Feeds: The foot point gives the pitch position, the torso gives the kit colour, the box becomes a thumbnail for the identity model. Measured: 89.7% of visible player time detected (IoU ≥ 0.5). Weakest on close-ups: 63.6% for boxes over 150 px tall. Note: About 14 outfield players are detected in each frame.
