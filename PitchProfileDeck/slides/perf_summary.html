About 60 seconds. One table for the whole system, every number measured on matches the models never trained on. Seeing and tracking are strong: the YOLOv8x detector finds 89.7% of visible player time, the kit-colour rule puts 97% of detected players in the right team, and 97.1% of tracked time is in tracks of a single player. The shirt-number reader gets about 80% of tracklets right. Our identity model is right 95% of the time it gives a name, but only about half of visible player time is named, so the verdict is partial. The T-DEED spotter has a mean average precision of 0.63 over 12 actions: very good on passes at 0.93, weak on tackles at 0.10. The hand-written event rules find passes with 68% precision and 70% recall. The spotter finds shots with 68% precision and 72% recall on unseen halves. The ball is the weakest link: the detector finds only 47% of visible balls. The practical consequence: team totals count every event whether or not a player can be named, so they are more reliable than player totals.
