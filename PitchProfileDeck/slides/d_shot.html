Removing the classifier changed no end-to-end score. More detail. Step 1: Candidates are ball releases within 45 m of the goal a team attacks: the end of a possession spell, or a fast ball towards goal that no touch explains. Step 2: Each candidate gets 26 features: distance and angle to goal, ball flight, defenders in the shooting cone, goalkeeper distance, what happened next, and broadcast editing. Step 3: Gradient-boosted trees (depth 3, learning rate 0.05, 250 iterations) score each candidate. Step 4: The decision threshold, 0.67, is set so that the shots called equal the shots labelled, because shot counts are statistics. Trained on: 18 analysed halves with 324 SoccerNet shot labels, 231 matched to a release. Checked: Leave-one-match-out on 9 games, then 4 unseen halves. Measured: On four unseen halves with 58 shot labels (within 2 s, same labels and matching for both): classifier 31 found from 92 calls (34% precision, 53% recall); T-DEED 42 found from 62 calls (68%, 72%). With each model at its best threshold, F1 is 0.45 against 0.71. The hand-written rule scores 25% / 21% on its own protocol. Role: Retired on 8 October 2026: the video spotter makes every shot call. Strongest features: The view leaving the pitch after the release, and the ball reaching the goal line.
