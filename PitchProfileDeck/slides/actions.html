Why a video model. Rules over 2D positions cannot tell tackles, blocks and headers from ordinary contacts, even on ground-truth positions. T-DEED watches the pixels and spots these, plus crosses, lofted passes, throw-ins and shots, with the acting team.

How we trained it. 3,000 steps on SoccerNet ball-action (5 games) and FOOTPASS (48 games). FOOTPASS classes act as partial labels; tackles and blocks are weighted up; early backbone stages are frozen.

About 75 seconds. The action spotter is the model that most changes what the app can report. Tackles, blocks and headers cannot be told from ordinary touches using 2D positions, even with perfect positions, so we use a model that watches the video. T-DEED takes a clip of 100 frames, about 8 seconds at 12.5 frames per second, passes it through a small RegNet-Y backbone with gate-shift temporal fusion and then a temporal encoder-decoder, and outputs a score per frame, per action class, with the team. We started from the published SoccerNet 2025 baseline and fine-tuned for 3,000 steps on SoccerNet ball-action spotting and 48 FOOTPASS games, treating FOOTPASS classes as partial labels, weighting tackles and blocks, and freezing the early stages. On held-out games mean average precision went from 0.48 to 0.60 on FOOTPASS and 0.61 to 0.63 on the SoccerNet test set; headers and blocks improved a lot, tackles are still weak, so we report tackles and blocks as expected counts. For shots the spotter beats our own gradient-boosted classifier, 0.71 and 0.70 against 0.36 and 0.47, so the classifier is only a fallback. Everything else on events is deliberately rules. The archetype profiler is built but not fitted yet because no players are labelled.
