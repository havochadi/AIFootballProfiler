About 75 seconds. This is how a tracklet gets a name. Because the broadcast cuts every few seconds, the shirt number is the only thing that reliably links a player across cuts, so we read it in two stages. First a ResNet-34 asks whether the number is legible at all; most thumbnails are not. Then PARSeq, a scene-text recogniser fine-tuned on SoccerNet, reads the digits from the readable ones, and we vote across up to 24 thumbnails per segment: 80% of 1,211 test tracklets read correctly. But numbers are only readable about half the time, so a third model covers the rest: a CLIP vision transformer we fine-tuned end to end on 6.0 million thumbnails of 1,485 real players from 48 FOOTPASS games. It has heads for the tens and units digit, a 256-dimensional appearance embedding trained with a contrastive loss where teammates are the negatives, and a goalkeeper flag. On three held-out games, appearance matching picked the right tracklets 98.2% of the time and goalkeepers were found with 90% recall at 99% precision. Overall about half of player time gets a name, and 95% of those names are right. Going from 19 to 48 training games lifted right-name time from 48.9% to 53.0%.
