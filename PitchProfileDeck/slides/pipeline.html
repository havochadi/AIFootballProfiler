About 70 seconds. Read it as three rows, left to right, with an arrow wherever one stage hands its result to the next. Each card poses one problem and names the answer to it: the model, rule or method we use. The video is the only input. The next slides take the problems in this order, one at a time. Stage 1 samples 12.5 frames a second. Stage 2, detect: YOLOv8x finds players, referees and the ball in each frame. Stage 3, track: BoT-SORT links boxes into tracks and a camera-cut detector makes sure nobody is followed across a cut. Stage 4, pitch map: an HRNet finds 57 points on the pitch lines and a homography turns pixels into metres. Stage 5, teams: K-means on shirt colour. Stage 6, ball path: a shortest-path search picks one ball per frame from many wrong candidates. Stage 7, identify: the shirt-number reader and our fine-tuned CLIP identity model turn tracks into named players. Stage 8, events: hand-written rules find passes and carries from positions, our fine-tuned T-DEED model finds shots, headers and tackles in the video. Stage 9 is arithmetic: distance, speed, heatmaps and counts. Green labels are pretrained models used as released, orange labels are models we fine-tuned or trained, grey ones are hand-written methods. The whole run takes about three quarters of the video length on one RTX 3090.
