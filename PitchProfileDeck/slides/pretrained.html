Four building blocks we did not train

Used as released

YOLOv8x detectors

Does: Finds players, goalkeepers and referees. A second detector finds the ball.

Comes from: Football weights from the Roboflow sports repository (Ultralytics YOLOv8x).

We use it: Runs on every sampled frame, 12.5 times a second.

Used as released

HRNetV2-W48 pitch keypoints

Does: Finds 57 points on the pitch lines in each camera view.

Comes from: No-Bells-Just-Whistles (Gutiérrez-Pérez and Agudo), trained on SoccerNet calibration data.

We use it: We fit a homography to turn pixels into metres, then clean the calibration over time.

Used as released

ResNet-34 + PARSeq number reader

Does: Checks whether a shirt number is readable, then reads its digits.

Comes from: The SoccerNet jersey-number pipeline (Koshkina and Elder; PARSeq by Bautista and Atienza).

We use it: Applied to the torso of each player thumbnail. Only readable thumbnails vote.

Method, no training

BoT-SORT tracker

Does: Links the boxes of one player from frame to frame.

Comes from: A standard tracker, run with camera-motion correction and no appearance model.

We use it: Our cut detector restarts every track at a camera cut.

Licences include AGPL-3.0, GPL-2.0 and CC BY-NC: fine for research, to be reviewed before commercial use

About 50 seconds. The first group of models we use exactly as published. YOLOv8x finds players, goalkeepers, referees and, with a second detector, the ball; it runs on every sampled frame. The HRNet from No-Bells-Just-Whistles finds 57 points on the pitch lines, and we add a homography so pixels become metres. The ResNet-34 and PARSeq pair from the SoccerNet jersey-number pipeline checks whether a number is readable and then reads it; only readable thumbnails vote. BoT-SORT is a standard tracker with no training at all; our own camera-cut detector restarts every track at a cut. Most of these licences are research-only or copyleft, which is fine for a course project but would need review before any commercial use.
