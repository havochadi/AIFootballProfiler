What it means

Finding players, teams and tracks is dependable. Naming varies with the camera: game 24 is detected 96% of the time but named only 30%.

About 50 seconds. The three numbers on top come from three unseen FOOTPASS games, 2.03 million true player-samples. 89.7% of the time a player is visible, he is detected. 97% of detected players get the right team, which comes from kit colour, so it rarely fails. 97.1% of tracked time sits in tracks that contain only one player, so a track is almost never two people mixed together. The chart splits the same measure by half: pale bar is detected, mid green is given any name, dark green is given the right name. Detection is steady at 82 to 96 percent. Naming swings from 26 to 64 percent because it depends on how close the camera is and whether shirt numbers face it: game 24 is detected 96% of the time but named only about 30%. The matrix on the right is a true confusion matrix for goalkeepers, over 13,610 tracklets from the three games: 616 of 685 goalkeepers are called correctly (recall 89.9%), and only 6 of 12,925 outfield players are wrongly called goalkeepers (precision 99.0%). Goalkeepers are easy because they wear a different kit. Models: detection is the YOLOv8x football detector, tracks come from BoT-SORT, teams from K-means on shirt colour, and the goalkeeper call and the names from our fine-tuned CLIP identity model.
