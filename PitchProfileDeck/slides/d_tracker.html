How do we follow one player through the match?

The problem

Detection looks at each frame alone, so nothing says two boxes show the same player. The camera also cuts about every 8 seconds, and a track must never carry on across a cut.

Our answer

Method, no training

BoT-SORT tracker + camera-cut rule

Data it uses: Player boxes from the detector, and a colour histogram of each frame.

Trained on: Nothing. Fixed settings, no training data.

Data it uses: Player boxes from the detector, and a colour histogram of each frame. Trained on: Nothing. Fixed settings, no training data. How it works: A Kalman filter predicts where each track goes next, with camera pans and zooms corrected. New boxes are matched to tracks by overlap, confident boxes first. A cut detector compares colour histograms: a distance above 0.35 is a cut. Training detail: Nothing to train. BoT-SORT is a standard tracker with fixed settings. The cut detector is hand-written, not a neural network. Outcome: 97.1% of tracked time in single-player tracks. The cut detector found 115 of 133 labelled hard cuts. The demo match has 730 cuts, about one every 8 seconds. More detail. Step 1: A Kalman filter predicts where each track will be in the next sample. Step 2: New boxes are matched to tracks by overlap: confident boxes first (score ≥ 0.25), weak ones second (≥ 0.1). Step 3: Camera pans and zooms are corrected first with sparse optical flow. Appearance matching is switched off. Step 4: A cut detector compares hue-and-saturation histograms of consecutive samples. A distance above 0.35 is a cut and tracking restarts. Step 5: Short tracks are joined into segments when they share a view and a team, the gap is at most 1.2 s and they are close enough. In → out: Boxes → tracks and segments. Origin: BoT-SORT is a standard tracker. The cut detector is hand-written, not a neural network. Settings: Match threshold 0.85. A track lost for about a second is dropped. Join distance at most 1.8 m + 7.5 m/s × gap. Measured: 97.1% of tracked time sits in single-player segments. The cut detector found 115 of 133 labelled hard cuts. Why it matters: A track never survives a camera cut. The demo match has 730 cuts, about one every 8 seconds, which is why an identity model is needed.
