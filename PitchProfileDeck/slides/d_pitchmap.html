About 60 seconds. How do we turn pixels into metres? The problem: Distance and speed need positions on the pitch, but the picture comes from a moving, zooming camera. Only a median 60% of broadcast time shows enough pitch lines to tell where the camera points. Our answer: HRNetV2-W48 pitch network + homography (used as released). Data it uses: One video frame at 960 × 540 px. Trained on: SoccerNet pitch images with labelled pitch points, from the original authors. Used as released. How it works: The network finds 57 known pitch points: corners, box corners, centre circle, penalty spots. RANSAC fits a homography from the matched points. Weak fits are rejected. Fits are cleaned: outliers dropped, gaps up to 1.2 s filled, then smoothed. Training detail: Not trained by us. No-Bells-Just-Whistles HRNetV2-W48 weights, used as released. Outcome: 0.18 m jitter after cleaning, down from 1.1 m. 90th percentile, fixed points. The limit: only a median 60% of broadcast time shows enough pitch to calibrate. More detail. Step 1: The frame is resized to 960 × 540. The network outputs 57 heat maps, one for each known pitch point: corners, box corners, centre circle, penalty spots. Step 2: The peak of each heat map, if confident (≥ 0.1486), gives that point’s position in the picture. Crossbar points are dropped. Step 3: RANSAC fits a homography from the matched points. It is kept only with at least 5 inliers within 6 px, an error under 4 px, and agreement with the painted lines. Step 4: Raw fits are cleaned: outliers more than 1 m from the running median are rejected, gaps up to 1.2 s are filled, and fits are smoothed over 5 samples. In → out: A frame → positions in metres on a 105 × 68 m pitch. Origin: No-Bells-Just-Whistles (Gutiérrez-Pérez and Agudo), used without retraining. Settings: Tried on every second sample and at the start of each camera shot. Measured: Jitter of fixed points projected to the pitch fell from 1.1 m to 0.18 m at the 90th percentile after cleaning. Limit: Only a median 60% of broadcast time (47–70% across 22 halves) shows enough pitch to calibrate.
