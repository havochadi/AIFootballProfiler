How do we keep a name across camera cuts?

The problem

Every camera cut ends all tracks, so a player comes back as a new, unnamed track. Names have to be rebuilt after each cut from up to 24 thumbnails per track, without joining two players who are on screen together.

Our answer

Fine-tuned by us

Grouping and naming rules

Data it uses: Up to 24 thumbnails per track, read by the identity model.

Trained on: No extra training. Rules tuned on 49 SoccerNet tracking clips and checked on FOOTPASS.

How it works

Our answer: Grouping and naming rules over the identity model (fine-tuned by us). Data it uses: Up to 24 thumbnails per track, read by the identity model. Trained on: No extra training. Rules tuned on 49 SoccerNet tracking clips and checked on FOOTPASS. How it works: Cut up to 24 thumbnails per track and combine their digit votes, weighted by confidence. Group segments into players by appearance, never joining two seen together. Name a group when one number holds at least 85% of its votes. Training detail: Uses the fine-tuned CLIP identity model from the previous slide. The grouping and naming rules are hand-written, with no extra training. Outcome: 95% of the names given are right. The right player is named for 52.3% of visible time. The unnamed half is not lost: unnumbered players keep their statistics. More detail. Step 1: Up to 24 thumbnails are cut from every track. Step 2: For each segment, the digit probabilities of its readable thumbnails (legibility ≥ 0.5) are combined, each weighted by how sure it is. Blurred or turned views count for almost nothing. Step 3: Segments are grouped into players by appearance, never joining two that are on screen together. Up to 15 s of overlap is tolerated. Step 4: A group is named when one number holds at least 85% of its votes. Groups with no reliable number stay as consistent unnumbered players. Goalkeepers: Found with 89.9% recall at 99.0% precision. Appearance: Matches a tracklet to the right player 98.3% of the time. Numbers: Right 94.2% of the time when confident, reading 38% of tracklets. Whole pipeline: 52.3% of visible player time given the right player; 95% of the names given are right. Note: The unnamed half is not lost: unnumbered players keep their statistics.
