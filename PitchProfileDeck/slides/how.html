How it works

From video to statistics in eight steps

1

Sample

12.5 frames per second from the video.

2

Detect, track

Players, referees and ball; tracks restart at camera cuts.

3

Map to pitch

Pitch keypoints turn pixels into metres on 105 × 68 m.

4

Teams

Kit colours split two teams; attack direction from positions.

5

Ball path

One most-likely ball path over the whole half.

6

Events

Passes by rules; shots and tackles by a video model.

7

Identity

Shirt numbers read; look-alikes matched across cuts.

8

Statistics

Per player and team: totals and rates per 90 minutes.

Only the video is used. No tracking feeds, event data or line-ups enter the analysis.

4

About 60 seconds. Walk left to right, top to bottom. Detection and tracking are the heavy GPU step. The pitch mapping is what lets us measure real distance and speed in metres instead of pixels. The broadcast cuts between cameras every few seconds, so identity is the hard part: a shirt number is the only thing that links a player across cuts, and we back it up with appearance matching. Events are a mix: simple rules over positions and the ball for passes and carries, and a video model for shots, tackles and headers. The key message in the footer: the video is the only input.
