What it means

The ball is found in under half of the frames where it is visible, and only 60% of broadcast time shows a usable pitch view. Everything built on them inherits both limits.

About 50 seconds. Left: a precision-recall curve for the ball detector on SoccerNet test clips that neither detector was trained on, 7,350 frames with the ball visible in 6,766. Each point is a confidence threshold. A better detector sits higher and further to the right. At the threshold of 0.1, the detector finds 47% of visible balls with 72% of its detections real. Right: four limits of the whole system. Across the 22 half-matches analysed so far, a median 60% of broadcast time shows a usable view of the pitch, from 47% to 70%; the ball is located in a median 64% of that, from 47% to 80%. So every per-player figure describes the time the player was on screen. Tackles: end to end on unseen games, one of 26 was found. And speed: processing takes about three quarters of the video length on one RTX 3090. Closing line: team totals count every found event whether or not a player can be named, so they are more reliable than player totals. Model: the curve is the YOLOv8x ball detector.
