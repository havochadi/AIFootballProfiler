How do we count passes and carries from positions?

The problem

Positions give only dots and a ball path. Nothing says what counts as a touch, a pass or a sprint.

Our answer

Hand-written rules

Event rules over positions

Data it uses: pitch positions of every player and the ball path, in metres, 12.5 times a second.

Trained on: nothing. The numbers were set and checked on one annotated SoccerTrack match.

Touch

The ball’s speed changes by at least 4 m/s while a player is within 2 m of it.

Possession

Touches by one player less than 2.5 s apart form one possession spell.

Pass

The gap between two spells in one camera view, at most 4 s. Same team: completed. Other team: intercepted.

Carry

A spell in which the player moved the ball at least 2 m between his first and last touch.

Shots

Not a rule: the video model (T-DEED) spots shots and credits them to the player at the ball.

Sprint

At least 3 steps in a row, about 1.2 s, at 25.2 km/h or faster.

About 60 seconds. The problem: positions give only dots and a ball path, and nothing says what counts as a touch, a pass or a sprint. Our answer is hand-written rules, not learned models. They read the pitch positions of every player and the ball path, in metres, 12.5 times a second, and they use no training data: just rules with numbers we set and checked against annotated matches. A touch is a sudden change in the ball’s speed of at least 4 metres a second with a player within 2 metres, because a ball only changes speed abruptly when something hits it. Touches by one player less than two and a half seconds apart form a possession spell. A pass is the gap between two spells in the same camera view, at most 4 seconds: if the next player is on the same team it is completed, otherwise intercepted. A carry is a spell in which the player moved the ball at least 2 metres. Shots are not rule-based: the video model spots them and credits the player at the ball. A sprint is at least three steps in a row, about 1.2 seconds, at 25.2 km/h or faster. For distance, positions are smoothed over 13 samples and summed in 0.4 second steps, dropping speeds above 11 metres a second. Tackles, blocks and headers are not rule-based, because they look like any other touch in position data, so the video model finds them.
