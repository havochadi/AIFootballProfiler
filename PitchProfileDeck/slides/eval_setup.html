Reading the charts that follow

Precision: of what the system reported, how much was real.

Recall: of what really happened, how much it found.

Average precision: precision averaged over every threshold. 1.0 is perfect.

Confusion matrix: counts of right and wrong calls. The diagonal is correct.

TP / FP / FN: found right / invented / missed.

Why it matters

None of the test matches were used to train or tune the model being measured, so these numbers show how it behaves on a new game.

About 40 seconds. Before any numbers, say how they were obtained. Six tests, each on data the models did not train on: players, teams and tracks on three unseen FOOTPASS games with ground truth for every player in every frame; shirt numbers on the SoccerNet Jersey test split; player identity on the same three FOOTPASS games; events from video on two unseen SoccerNet games; the event rules on one annotated SoccerTrack match, using the annotated positions so the rules are tested without the vision errors; and the ball detector on SoccerNet test clips. Then define the words used on the next slides: precision is how much of what we report is real, recall is how much of the real events we find, average precision combines the two over every threshold, and a confusion matrix is simply the counts of right and wrong calls. Caveat to say if asked: the event-rule test is one match, and the spotter thresholds per class were picked to maximise F1 on the same test games, which flatters precision and recall slightly; average precision does not depend on a threshold. Models under test: YOLOv8x with BoT-SORT and kit-colour K-means for players, teams and tracks; ResNet-34 plus PARSeq for shirt numbers; a fine-tuned CLIP ViT-B/16 for identity; the fine-tuned T-DEED spotter for events from video; hand-written rules for passes and carries from positions; YOLOv8x for the ball.
